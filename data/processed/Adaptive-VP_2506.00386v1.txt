A.2 IncorporatingLiterature
The following describes types of problematic patients who create difficulties in
nurse-patient communication:
Type 1: Overly Dependent Patients
Attempt to resolve psychological fears about their illness by becoming excessively
dependent on nurses
Individuals who worry excessively about their illness or use the nurse call button
too frequently
Type 2: Overly Authoritative Patients
Attempt to manipulate doctors through threats or inducing guilt
Feel threatened by nurses having power over their life and death, and thus believe
they have the right to express anger and make excessive or inappropriate demands
as a defense mechanism
Patients who never praise or thank nurses, are obsessed with filing lawsuits
against nurses, or constantly complain
Type 3: Threatening and Violent Patients
Patients who argue with others
Patients who express anger and hostility
Patients who are violent towards their family members or objects
Patients who threaten or show violent behavior towards nurses
Type 4: Non-compliant Patients
Patients who are excessively pessimistic about treatment or directly engage in
behaviors that interfere with treatment

Continued smoking by lung cancer patients
Continued drinking by alcoholic patients
While overly dependent, they use defense mechanisms to deny their chances of survival
Give up hope of being treated and take pride in self-destructive behaviors
Actually derive satisfaction from interfering with treatment
